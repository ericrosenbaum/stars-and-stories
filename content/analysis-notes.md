# Analysis notes — research groundwork for the Stars & Stories deep analysis

Working notes behind `content/world-dna.md` (the essay published on the site's
`#analysis` page). Not published to the site — `tools/build-site.ts` copies only
`world-dna.md`. Prepared 2026-07-06 from a full-corpus ingestion: every one of the
193 transcripts was read (12 chronological batches), plus a quantitative pass over
all `story.json` files and a literature review of primary sources.

## Corpus facts

- 193 transcribed stories, 2024-09-20 → 2026-07-06 (Izzy born 2022-08-20: ages 2;1 → 3;10)
- ~218,800 transcript words total: Izzy 65,872 (30.1%), Dad 152,916
- Izzy's mean words per turn: ~5 at age 2;1 → ~13 at 3;4–3;7 (peak month 2026-01: 16.8)
- Izzy's word share: ~23–31% through age 2 → 40–48% in early 2026
- Longest single Izzy turn: 163 words (Late Night Tea with Mrs. Piggy, 2026-01-09, age 3;4)
- Highest Izzy word share: 90% (The Puking Cats Anthem, 2025-09-12)
- "Once upon a time" first appears in Izzy's own speech at exactly age 3;0 (2025-09-20)
- Reported-speech markers (said/says/asked…) in Izzy's turns: ~34% of stories at 3;0 → 95% by 3;7–3;10
- "Because" in Izzy's turns: rises to 84% of stories by age 3;7–3;10; "suddenly" first at 3;7
- Character continuity spans: Hattie the Mouse 2;6 → 3;10 (16 months, 53 stories incl. "Haddy");
  Seeker 48 stories from 3;1; Mischief Mouse from 2;4; Nana across the whole corpus

## Research questions (developed from literature + corpus sampling)

1. **The great handover.** How does Dad's scaffolding change as Izzy's competence
   grows, and when does she seize the director's chair? (Vygotsky's zone of
   proximal development; Bruner/Wood's scaffolding and its "contingent withdrawal";
   Whitehurst's dialogic reading.)
2. **Climbing the narrative ladder.** Where does Izzy sit on Applebee's stages of
   narrative (heaps → sequences → primitive narratives → chains → true narratives)
   at each age, in her own words?
3. **Voices in her mouth.** When do quoted speech, dialogue tags ("said Hattie"),
   in-character performance, and stories-within-stories emerge?
4. **Is this a paracosm?** Does the story-world meet Cohen & MacKeith's definition
   of a paracosm — a persistent imagined world with consistent rules, geography,
   and canon, maintained over months and defended against violation? (Notable:
   paracosms are classically documented from about age 7 up; Root-Bernstein links
   childhood "worldplay" to adult creativity.)
5. **The word forge.** What do her invented words and chants (Frasgle, Bissolam,
   snergle, "Kamoose-kajuice-kaboose") show about phonological play — and why is
   rhyme the corpus's law of magic? (Chukovsky's From Two to Five; phonological
   awareness research.)
6. **Life into myth.** How does real life — a new baby sister, potty training,
   puking cats, snowstorms, a fossil kit — get metabolized into story? (Engel's
   The Stories Children Tell; Nelson's Narratives from the Crib.)
7. **The soft landing.** How do the stories function as bedtime emotional
   regulation — cozy endings, fear handled inside the fiction, coping scripts
   spoken by characters? (Paley on story as the child's thinking; attachment and
   bedtime-routine research.)

## Answers in brief (full-corpus evidence)

1. **Handover.** Age 2;1: pure slot-filling ("It was driving." — "Where was it
   going?" — "It was driving to Monica… No... Nana's house"). By 2;4 she names
   protagonists and rejects premises ("No they didn't start with a problem"). At
   2;7 the signature meta-move hardens — "Keep telling the story and then you'll
   find out" — used across dozens of stories to control pacing while withholding
   plot; she also starts interrogating Dad with wh-questions (role reversal). By
   3;7 the flip is total: she dictates lines for Dad to repeat ("Once upon a time.
   Now repeat." / "No, say once upon a time") and by 3;10 delegates narration
   ("And what did the bunny say?") — the child scaffolds the adult. She even
   reflects on the division of labor: "Actually, I think you are the one because
   you're very good at Seeker and I'm not very good because I have a life of
   stuff you know" (3;9).
2. **Narrative ladder.** Labels/sequences at 2;1 → explicit problem statements at
   2;4 ("The problem was his bookcase was broken and it had a hole in it") →
   argued story grammar at 2;6 ("No, that's not the beginning! The beginning is
   she said 'Hmm, I'm going to build a tall tower'") → first fully-formed solo
   story at exactly 3;0 ("Once upon a time there was a mouse named Mickey. He was
   away from her house. She had a big adventure so she didn't have to worry. The
   end.") → complications ("There was only one little problem"), mysteries solved
   by clue-deduction, and multi-strand conflict-resolution with moral reasoning by
   3;8–3;10 ("I'm just a fish, I don't have arms like you do… that's why I wander
   around eating other people's kelp").
3. **Voicing.** Dialogue tags from 2;4 ("You could! Of course! said his dad"),
   routine tagged speech through age 2, full in-character performance at 3;4
   (Muppets story: voices Mrs. Piggy telling a story about her own sister — a
   story-within-a-story with a stated "happy ending"), invented-language
   translation ("Meow meow marbel... That means I got my claw stuck"), and by 3;10
   nested genealogy-tales ("Ancient Hattie the Mouse… dodging the dinosaurs").
4. **Paracosm: yes, by the book.** Persistent characters over 16 months; fixed
   physical laws articulated by Izzy herself ("Potions are something you drink
   and then you say a rhyme and then they teleport you somewhere. That's how they
   work"); stable geography blending real and imagined (Cornell Street sits on
   the flight path to the Planet of the Mookers; "I live in Roslindale,
   remember?"); active canon defense ("No, not blue fur, orange fur"; "No, made
   by Isabella. Silver Cat can't make potions"; "Anything means anything. Chicky
   can only understand his flashing lights"); cross-story callbacks ("Now I'm not
   allergic to coral anymore"); story arcs sustained across nights (Wobbly
   Whisker's lost mother, three stories).
5. **Word forge.** Naming litanies at 2;4 (Shriggy/Tiggy/Pliggy/Jiggy/Riggy);
   metalinguistic play at 2;7 ("brio neo… That means nothing!"; "Abarium?
   Aquarium. Abarium? No, aquarium."); made-up words as the actual magic system
   from 2;11 ("Baskamadasca, jiggidy jaska" summons mice; travel rhymes
   "Higher and higher to the plane of fire," "Kamoose-kajuice-kaboose"); at 3;3
   she pitches a coinage in-character and it becomes canon ("'…I have one in mind
   that I'd like to use: mooska-kadooska,' said Seeker"); live rhyme-workshopping
   at 3;5 ("Siffle Piffle Pip... No, Siffle Piffle Pup... No, Sniffle... No,
   Siffle Papiffle").
6. **Life into myth.** Baby sister Avery's naming (2;1) and arrival (2;7); potty
   training as "potty suits" and Seeker with pee on his hands; the real cats'
   vomiting immortalized as The Puking Cats Anthem (90% Izzy-authored, 3;0);
   new-school anxiety ("She didn't eat snack or take a nap. She just cried and
   waited for her mom"); sibling toy-fights processed via Mrs. Piggy; a fossil
   kit becomes the Prehistoric Potion; snowstorm prep, dentist visits, slime
   recipes, birthday-turning-four.
7. **Soft landing.** Coping scripts inside fiction from 2;7 ("Take a deep breath
   and count to four"; "But don't be really mad Hattie. All of your feelings are
   okay"); bravery tech (superhero costumes, magic flashlight against the
   Deep-Deep Dark she named herself as "a little bit scared" of at 2;6);
   scary missions rescheduled to morning; villains reformed by valentines; at
   3;10 she runs the ritual — lullaby to Dad-as-Seeker ("New places are hard.
   When you go to sleep, it feels more better, when you tell me why") and
   theorizes which stories are sleep-safe ("stories might make everyone wide
   awake… they tell what they're imagining… that might distract you from
   sleeping"). One honest limit case: at 3;6 the frame fails ("But I want to do
   beads. I'm disappointed. I just want to do beads").

## Primary sources

(Key empirical claims verified via an adversarial deep-research pass — each claim
below marked "verified" survived 3-vote refutation attempts against the primary
source. Book-level characterizations of Cohen & MacKeith, Root-Bernstein,
Chukovsky, Paley, and Nelson are standard readings of those works but were not
web-verified in that pass.)

Verified findings worth keeping:
- Scaffolding's three defining features — contingency, fading, transfer of
  responsibility — are the van de Pol, Volman & Beishuizen (2010, Educational
  Psychology Review) synthesis; Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976) coined the metaphor
  but did not cite Vygotsky — the ZPD link was made retrospectively (Cazden 1979,
  Wertsch 1985). [verified]
- Haden, Haine & Fivush (1997, Dev. Psych. 33, 15 families at 40 & 70 months):
  parents' provision of narrative structure INCREASED across the preschool years
  — adults escalate rather than withdraw structural input; maternal emphasis on
  evaluations predicted children's later narrative structure. [verified]
- Cremin, Flewitt, Swann, Faulkner & Kucirkova (2018, J. Early Childhood
  Research 16(1)): even "verbatim" adult scribes co-author children's dictated
  stories ("neatens up" hesitations); children control narration pacing via gaze
  — co-narration is jointly steered, multimodally. [verified]
- Nicolopoulou et al. (2015, Early Childhood Research Quarterly): year-long RCT,
  149 Head Start preschoolers — Paley-style storytelling + story-acting improved
  narrative comprehension (d = .35); number of stories told predicted gains.
  McNamee et al. (1985): dictation alone is insufficient; enactment matters.
  [verified]
- Whitehurst et al. (1988): dialogic reading raised expressive language and MLU
  vs. controls. [verified]
- Bloom, Lahey, Hood, Lifter & Fiess (1980, J. Child Language 7, four children
  aged 2–3): "and" is the first syntactic connective, and its meanings unfold in
  the order Additive < Temporal < Causal < Adversative — a yardstick for Izzy's
  connective growth ("because" in 84% of stories by 3;7–3;10). [verified]
- Haight & Miller (1992/1993, nine families, 12–48 months): early pretend play
  is predominantly social — parent-child co-pretending is the normative context
  for early imagination. [verified]
- Engel (1995): thesis that "in constructing stories, children are constructing
  themselves" (ch. 7: "We are the stories we tell"). [verified]

## Method

- Quantitative: `corpus-stats` script over all 193 `story.json` files (per-month
  Izzy word share, mean words/turn, feature first-occurrences, character spans).
- Qualitative: 12 parallel readers, each reading every transcript in a
  chronological batch, tagging evidence against the 7 research questions and
  returning verbatim quotes; synthesized into the essay.
- All quotes in the essay were verified verbatim against transcripts.
