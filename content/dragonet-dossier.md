# The Dragonets — Evidence Dossier

*Compiled from the Stars & Stories transcript archive (203 stories, 2025-02 → 2026-08) for
D&D campaign use. Everything in Parts I–III is grounded in a direct transcript line; where a
claim is inference or where the archive contradicts itself, it is labelled. Part IV lists the
silences — the places where you are free to invent because canon says nothing.*

# Part I — What the transcripts establish

## 1. Taxonomy: a dragonet is a small *kind* of dragon, not a young dragon

This is the single most load-bearing fact in the archive, and it is stated flatly, twice, in
two different stories.

> "He is a dragon, but he's something called a **dragonet**. They are very small, and Dimitar
> himself is a lovely shade of purple."
> — Seeker, *The Giant Rescue on Volcano Island* (t≈146)

> "**Oh my goodness! Are you a baby? What kind of dragon are you?**" [asks the giant ice
> dragon] And Dimitar said, "**Nope, I'm a grown-up. I'm very old, actually, thank you very
> much. I'm a dragonet, which is a small kind of dragon.**"
> — *Dimitar and the Castle of Ice Fangsicle*

The mistake is played as a recurring social indignity: big creatures read dragonets as
hatchlings. Dimitar corrects them every time, and the correction is a characterisation beat —
he is touchy about it. Also note that a full-grown ancient ice dragon had *never heard of
dragonets*: "**What's a dragonet?** said Ice Fangsicle." They are obscure, insular, and
regional.

## 2. Size

Never given in feet. Established only by comparison, which is arguably better for a DM:

- Tiny beside a great dragon: Ice Fangsicle is "**a giant ancient white dragon the size of a
  school bus, he dwarfed me. I had to look straight up into his face**"; he has to bring "his
  head down low to the ground to squint close at Dimitar" (*Castle of Ice Fangsicle*).
- Small beside a dwarf wizard: Seeker embiggened to "**three times as tall as the entire
  castle**" has to warn, "**I don't want to step on any tiny dragonets**" (*Giant Rescue*).
- Small beside a human adult: a dragonet to Miss Thereso — "**you're really tall compared to
  us. Maybe you could help us by, I don't know, finding us a shovel or something**"
  (*Underground Rescue*, t≈898).
- Comparable to a mouse's world: Dimitar shares a single fish with Hattie the Mouse as a meal
  for two, argues over who gets the head vs. the tail, and can be knocked back by a mouse
  eating "the body and the tail" of his dinner (*Book World*, *Rainbow Fruit World*).
- Roughly cat-sized or smaller: fits into cat-school furniture, can be trapped in a hole by a
  single house cat, hides *behind Seeker's back* (*Underground Rescue*).
- But: a dragonet-sized creature can nonetheless carry a mouse and haul a bucket, and Dimitar
  once carried a passenger on his back in flight (*Blueberry Dragon Pop*).

Practical read: **Small size, roughly cat-to-badger mass, in a world where mice and cats are
people.** They live in stone castles with human-scale doors because everything on the Circular
Island is built at their scale.

## 3. Lifespan and growth

> "I should let you know that **I'm quite old, actually. I'm even older than Seeker. I'm older
> than most grown-ups. Because we dragonets, we live a long time. I'm over 200 years old. So
> I'm not going to get any bigger. I'm my full size. I may look small, but I have a lot of
> years in me. And I know a lot of things. I've been a lot of places.**"
> — Dimitar, *Castle of Ice Fangsicle*

- 200+ years and full-grown; a dragonet's small stature is terminal, not developmental.
- Aging shows in colour: "**you can see mine's purple, although it's faded a little bit
  because I am, to be honest, very old**" (*Sardine Bakery*, t≈91).
- Compare the big dragons' clock: Icicle is "**497 years old, but once I was four when I was a
  tiny little dragon**" (*Rainbow Party*, t≈119). Same order of magnitude, so dragonets are
  not a short-lived offshoot — they are long-lived *and* small.
- Yet dragonets have living mothers who pick them up from school and arrange playdates (§9).
  Canon does not reconcile this. A 200-year-old at nursery school is either a joke, a hint
  that dragonet schooling is lifelong, or (my recommendation for play) evidence that dragonet
  society treats age as irrelevant to status and keeps its members enrolled in institutions
  forever.

## 4. Hatching and origin

The only hatching account is a story-within-a-story: Papa Mole reads it to his mole children
out of "**the ancient book about Dimitar**", found dusty in his attic, cover art of "**a tiny
purple dragon named Dimitar**" (*Dimitar and the Blueberry Dragon Pop*, 2025-10-21). So in the
world, **Dimitar is a legend with published books about him**, and his hatching is deep
history read aloud at bedtime — which is how a 200-year-old can also be a newborn on the page.

From that account:

- A "**great dragon nest**" high in cold mountains, containing "**many broken eggshells from
  the dragons who had already hatched**" — and one egg "**smaller than all the rest. Much,
  much smaller**", unhatched. Dragonets may hatch as the runt of an ordinary dragon clutch.
- Nest materials (Izzy's invention, and gorgeous): "**strawberries and feathers, with gold …
  gold, sparkly glitter dragon branches … and sea grass and dragon pests. Dragon pests are a
  kind of rock.**"
- Newly hatched: "**the little tiny purple face of a very smart and very mad-looking little
  dragon**", speaking "**baby dragon language**" that translates to "**I need some snacks right
  away!**"
- Immediate competence: no parent present, opinions about food already formed, flight untested
  but assumed — "**Well, I think I can fly. I've never tried it yet**" — and it works first try.
- He was wrong about his own future: "**No, I'm gonna grow up to be a bigger dragon. I don't
  think I'm gonna be really big, but I'm gonna be medium-sized, probably.**" A poignant hook:
  dragonet hatchlings may not know they are dragonets.
- Hatchlings leave immediately and choose their own territory: "**I can't stay here in the
  nest. I have to go fly and find a new place to have a nest.**" He picked a jungle off a map.

## 5. Body, senses, and what they lack

- **Scales, worn bare.** "**I and my dragonet friends, we all just flap around in our scaly
  skin.**" (*Sardine Bakery*) Dragonets wear no clothing anywhere in the archive, in a world
  where mice run hat shops. Their scales shed water: "**You're just wearing your scales and
  those just drip dry right off, don't they?**" (*Gemstone Reef*)
- **Tail: rough, scaly, brown/purple.** Petting is a greeting ritual with cats. Ginger: "Want
  to feel my tail?" / Dimatar: "**your tail is so soft. Want to feel my tail?**" / Izzy: "**Scaly
  and brown.**" / "**Yeah, my tail is scaly because I'm a dragonet.**" (*Ginger Catasorry*,
  t≈915–934). He notes he "**haven't had a tail pet in a long time**" — touch-starved,
  socially isolated species.
- **Sharp teeth, used as tools.** "**You know dragonettes have very sharp teeth. I could just
  bite it. Here, watch this. Chomp!**" — biting a bead-string to length (*Flight to Cadasori*,
  t≈257). Also eats by putting "**his whole face into a bowl of dried crunchy fish**".
- **Claws that snap as a summons.** "**Dimitar snapped one of his claws like this — snap,
  snap**" and two orange dragonet servants sprint for snacks (*Giant Rescue*, t≈1300). He does
  it again to muster the entire castle. Snapping is command authority.
- **Excellent nose, described as lizard-like.** "**Dimatar lifted his little lizard-like nose
  up in the air and did a sniff-sniff-sniff**" — he smells a bakery from another street
  (*Sardine Bakery*, t≈158). Seeker defers to it: "**You have a better nose than I do,
  Dimitar. Do you smell anything?**" (*Rainbow Fruit World*).
- **Wings, arms, legs, tail, one eye per side, mouth with teeth** — from Dimitar drawing his
  own portrait at school: "**I'm trying to draw a little dragon, like me. Dragonet. See there's
  its mouth with the teeth and a little eye and wings and arms and legs and a tail.**"
  (*Ginger Catasorry*, t≈654)
- **A wing substitutes for a hand.** Asked to raise his hand at school: "**I'll raise my
  wing.**" (*Strawberry Island*, t≈201) He also pets a cat's tail with a wing on request.
- **No breath weapon. No magic. No fire.** In an archive where ice dragons freeze islands and
  a fire dragon "**sneezes a big fireball**", *no dragonet ever breathes anything*. Dragonets
  travel by borrowed wizard potions and other people's flying ladders. This is the sharpest
  design line in the material: **dragonets are the dragons without the dragon powers.**
- Their only offensive display is **bluff** — see §8.

## 6. Flight

- Verb of choice is "flap": "**They flap! Flap, flap, flap, flap, flap, flap, flap. That means
  flying.**" (*Flight to Cadasori*, t≈62)
- Faster than ground travel: asked "**who is faster? The flying dragonettes or the driving
  cats?**" Izzy first says cats, then corrects — "**no, they are slower, the dragons are
  faster**" — and the dragonets overfly the cats' car en route to school.
- Vertical, agile, and used casually indoors and out: Dimitar "**flapped up high into the air
  and circled around once, whoosh! all the way around the castle, and then zoomed off down the
  mountain**" just to fetch a snack (*Ice Festival*, t≈207).
- Hunting stoop: over a blueberry ocean "**he flew up high and then dove straight down and with
  a splash … dove down and then swooped back up again**" holding a lemon fish (*Rainbow Fruit
  World*, t≈116).
- Can ferry a passenger: a mouse rides on his back on his very first flight (*Blueberry Dragon
  Pop*).
- Aerial play is the standard dragonet recreation: two dragonets "**zoomed into the air! They
  zoomed this way and that way, and the teacher said, wow, that is making me dizzy**"
  (*Strawberry Island*, t≈557). Village dragonets are seen "**playing in the air, flapping
  around and chasing each other and laughing**".
- **Tiring on long hauls.** Crossing to the mountains, "**it was a long, long journey and my
  wings became very tired**" — and coming home he asks to ride *on the big dragon's back*
  (*Castle of Ice Fangsicle*). Range is limited; a dragonet expedition is a logistics problem.
- Long distances are otherwise done by **ship** (a pirate ship, *Floofer Kaploofer*), Seeker's
  **skyship**, or the **magic flying ladder**.

## 7. Diet — fish, absolutely and comically

The most consistently attested cultural fact in the entire dragonet corpus.

- Species-level statement, from Izzy: "**They like to eat fishies and ice.**" (*The Dragonettes
  and the Rainbow Forest Quest*, t≈301)
- Staple: dried fish. "**These are some little bowls full of delicious crunchy things that we
  dragonets like to eat. They're actually dried fish … It's quite delicious, and a little
  salty. Make sure you get some water too.**" (*Giant Rescue*, t≈1353)
- Exclusivity, stated by Dimitar himself: "**Oh, wait! Save some for me. That's all I really
  eat.**" and, offered cat kibble, "**Crunch crunch. Blech! This is disgusting! How do you eat
  this stuff? I mean, I only like fish, I guess.**" (*Ginger Catasorry*, t≈943)
- Fish-derived everything: sardine cupcakes with "salty sardine filling", "fish coffee with
  milk", fish crackers, a fish sandwich, "fish juice", "fish sauce", a "fish-flavored cupcake
  with fishy flavored sprinkles", a requested "herring roll" and "salmon-flavored cake"
  (*Sardine Bakery*; *Strawberry Island*; *Ginger Catasorry*).
- **Fish anatomy is etiquette.** Splitting one fish: "**Okay, I get the head.**" / "**the head
  is delicious but you can have the head and I'll have the tail**" (*Rainbow Fruit World*,
  t≈43). The grievance that starts the Book World story is that Hattie "**took a big bite and
  ate all of the body and the tail**", leaving Dimitar "**only had the head left to eat**".
  Head and tail are separately desirable portions; taking both is a real insult.
- Live catch is fair game and dragonets are frank predators — Dimitar's first reaction to a
  reef is "**I wonder if we could eat some of these fish, they look delicious**" (*Shark
  Friends*), and he asks a fruit-person permission before eating a lemon fish. As a hatchling
  he asked outright, "**Hey, I like mice. Could I eat that mouse?**" — a line worth keeping in
  mind given he later *marries* a mouse (§10).
- Hatchling palate trials (all rejected): leaf, stick, flower, mud. Accepted: "**dragon
  water**" and a "**blueberry raisin dragon pop**" — "**it's the same color as me! I'm purple
  and it's purple … so cold and refreshing and sweet!**"
- **Ice is a delicacy, and the reason for the archive's first dragonet quest.** The Mayor sends
  Dimitar to fetch ice "**for our summer festival to cool down our delicious drinks**".
- Salt is prized. The frozen-island rescue ends in "**a huge feast of all sorts of delicious
  salty snacks**", though Dimitar objects to salt as a *tool*: "**With seasoning? Everything's
  going to taste like salt!**"
- They will nibble the scenery. As conductor of a cheese train: "**You may take a little
  nibble, but don't eat up the whole cheese train, 'cause then it won't be able to take
  passengers somewhere.**" (*Cheese Beach*, t≈133)

## 8. Temperament

Dragonets are consistently written as **grumpy, proud, possessive, forgetful, loud, easily
panicked, and quick to reconcile**. This is the tonal core and it is very usable.

- **Bluffing menace.** A lone dragonet meeting strangers puffs himself up by faking a giant's
  tread: "**the sound of a tiny dragonet pretending to be a big dragon by making mouth noises
  as it walked … and looked at Seeker and Hattie very meanly. 'Who are you?'**" The instant a
  mutual friend is named, "**his whole attitude changed … he looked happy**" (*Giant Rescue*,
  t≈633–727). First contact with dragonets should always be a threat display that collapses on
  a password.
- **Vanity.** An earthquake knocks a picture off his wall: "**Oh, that was one of my favorite
  pictures of me. Could somebody put it back up, please?**"
- **Possessiveness, with a stated wound underneath.** On his library: "**You can't have any of
  them, Hattie!**"; and the reason — "**I lent one of my dragonet books to my dragonet friend
  and he was trying to read it while he was flying, and he was flying over the ocean and he
  dropped it in the ocean! … I even dove down and found the book but it was all wet and then it
  all just turned to mush.**" (*Book World*, t≈308)
- **Forgetfulness.** Mid-quest: "**Oh, oh yeah. Wait, what's our mission again?**" (*Floofer
  Kaploofer*, t≈347). His own assistant's assessment: "**I'm Dimitar's assistant. That means I
  help him out with everything. And boy does he need a lot of help with everything.**"
- **Collective panic and instant recovery.** Told the castle must be moved, "**all the little
  dragonets were very upset. They said, 'What? What? What are you talking about? Move the
  castle? The castle's here, it's never moving anywhere, it's a castle made of stone, that's
  ridiculous. Why would we need to do that anyway? Oh yeah, we do need to do that anyway
  because the volcano might erupt. Okay, let's do it.'**" (*Giant Rescue*, t≈1952) — an entire
  town's deliberation in nine seconds. Likewise the emergency chorus in *Underground Rescue*:
  "**Hey, no, what do you… What should we do? Oh, no, don't bother everyone. I'm not sure who.
  I'm scared. Wait, but, but we can figure this out. But no, no, help. We should go home right
  now.**"
- **Flight, not fight.** Facing the cat who imprisoned them, Zara and Ginger "**just ran … and
  hid behind his back**" — then hugged him once he apologised.
- **Bickering as a mode of friendship.** Dimitar's two documented arguments are both with his
  wife, both over food or books, and both resolved by third-party mediation from Seeker with
  explicit needs-based negotiation ("**you both have things that you need, and maybe if you
  tell each other what you need…**").
- **Curiosity and appetite for learning, despite the grumbling.** "**This Catasorry school is a
  lot of work, but I like learning.**" He asks to keep the shiny beads and is refused.

## 9. Society, settlement, and institutions

**The Perfectly Circular Island** is the dragonet homeland in the current era.

- A perfect circle of land with one central mountain that is really **a volcano**; sparkling
  yellow beaches, forests, little rivers, and dragonet villages. Dimitar's stone castle sits on
  the volcano's flank, with a **bell tower** whose giant bell doubles as his doorbell.
- Population: "**Dragonets! Tiny dragons … lots of little dragonets of all different colors
  living on this island**", in "**little houses**", "**sitting in chairs, sipping on drinks, or
  working in their gardens, growing things to eat**" (*Giant Rescue*, t≈934). Dozens live in
  Dimitar's castle alone. Icicle refers to "**all the villages of the dragonets that live on
  his island with him**" (*Salty Solution*, t≈477).
- So: **an agrarian village culture with a fortified centre, gardens, drinks on the porch, and
  a household staff.**
- Household hierarchy is real but informal: Pouska is Dimitar's **assistant** and escort who
  hands guests off with "**Okay, this is as far as I take you. Good luck with Dimitar, ha-ha!**";
  two **orange dragonets** are kitchen staff who also do carpentry (one hammers the picture
  back onto the wall with "a little tiny hammer").
- **Dragonet Town**, in a jungle "**in a very warm part of the world**", is Dimitar's *earlier*
  home (the jungle he chose as a hatchling) and has a **Mayor** who assigns civic quests, plus a
  **summer festival** with cold drinks. Warm-climate jungle towns and a temperate island: two
  documented dragonet settlement types.
- **Mail and messages matter.** The Giant Rescue is triggered by a letter on purple paper
  ("**Me and all of my dragonet friends are in a real pickle. Faithfully, your old friend
  Dimitar**"). The Underground Rescue is triggered by a written distress note that arrives
  **torn in half and carried in two dragonets' mouths** — "**it says, 'Help. We're trapped
  underground.'**" Dragonet emergency comms are physical, fragile, and garbled.
- **Doorbells are a civilisational obsession** across the archive — every dragonet home has
  one, ringing it is the standard story-opening, and Dimitar's is a tower bell that goes "bing
  bong" loud enough to annoy him.
- **Festivals and parties**: the jungle summer festival; the island salt-feast; a rainbow disco
  birthday attended by a delegation of dragonets; and Dimitar as the one who calls "**Hey,
  everybody, time for the dance party!**"
- No evidence of hoards, treasure, currency, kings, priests, armies, or war. Governance
  attested: **one mayor, one castle-holder, and a lot of shouting.**

## 10. Family, kinship, and marriage

- **Mothers are present and named.** Dimitar's mother appears twice under two different names —
  "**Dimetar's mom's name is Demora**" (*Strawberry Island*, t≈707) and "**said Dimatar's mom,
  Shiliko**" (*Sardine Bakery*, t≈642). She walks him to school, picks him up ("**Oh hi mom,
  let's go home**"), and negotiates with other mothers.
- **Cross-island playdate diplomacy** between mothers, verbatim: "**We live on Circular Island.
  What island does your family live on?**" / "**Strawberry Island.**" / "**Do you think we could
  come over sometime for a playdate?**" / "**Of course.**"
- **Dragonets marry outside their species.** Seeker, mediating: "**But you two are married, and
  so it's very important for you to learn how to share things, Dimitar and Hattie.**" (*Book
  World*, t≈219). Hattie the Mouse co-resides in Dimitar's castle (place record: "the weathered
  stone fortress where Dimitar and Hattie reside"), and Seeker reminds them mid-argument to
  "**remember that you love each other**". Interspecies marriage is normal and unremarked.
- Precedent among big dragons too: **Uncle Bill is married to Icicle** the ice dragon,
  confirmed by Izzy (*Star World*, t≈132).
- **"Family" is joinable and chosen.** Offered friendship by the reformed villain, the rescued
  dragonet Zara instead declares: "**No, I wanna be part of Dimitar's family.**" (*Underground
  Rescue*, t≈2241) Dragonet kinship looks adoptive/affiliative — a household you petition into.
- Dimitar has no children in the archive. "Dragoness", the mother-dragon of the origin nest, is
  named once and never characterised.

## 11. Schooling — the strongest institution in the material

Dragonets and cats attend school **together**. It is called **Cadasori** / **Ginger
Catasorry** / "Ginger school", and Izzy is always the teacher. Its location wobbles across
tellings — the Circular Island place record calls the island "the hallowed grounds of the
school", Dimitar and his mother *fly* there and land in a parking lot, and Izzy at one point
says flatly that it is "in my room".

> "**That means it's a school for cats and dragonets. There's a lot of dragonet students
> here.**" — *Dimatar's Big Day at Ginger Catasorry* (t≈207)

- **Curriculum**, all attested: the planet map (continents *and* planets — Asia, Africa, North
  and South America, Europe, Australia; Venus, Saturn, Jupiter, Earth, Mars, the Moon), drawing
  lessons, bead counting and sorting by colour, beadwork/necklaces, circle time with songs,
  nap time, snack time (kibble for cats, fish for dragonets), lunch, and outside time.
- **Multi-species architecture.** "**There's steps and a handrail or wing-rail. There's a
  handrail and a wing-rail and a paw-rail.**" (t≈2156) Dad's reply is the design principle:
  "**That's so convenient for all the different kind of creatures that want to be on the
  playground.**"
- **Institutional details**: lunchboxes required except on "**Buy-days**"; "music Wednesdays";
  placemats chosen from a cart; nap bag, mat, and stuffie (Dimitar's is a purple cat named
  Sanina); the teacher covers him with "**his blanket with dragons**"; the teacher's flashlight
  is not for students.
- **Dragonets are provincial.** Shown a world map: "**I've never heard of any of these places
  … I just live on the Perfectly Circular Island**", and later, "**there's no circular island
  on here**". A dragonet's mental map is his island. Only Dimitar, the widely-travelled
  exception, knows better — and even he had "**never been underwater like this**" the first
  time and had "**never seen a beach like that**" off-island.
- **Literacy is real and prestigious.** Dimitar keeps a **library** of books specifically
  "**about dragonets**"; Hattie the Mouse by contrast "**didn't know how to read yet**". There
  is a dragonet literature, and losing a book is a genuine tragedy.

## 12. Health and vulnerability

- **Allergic to volcanic lava and magma** — this is why Dimitar is bedridden after the eruption
  scare: "**he was allergic to the volcano's lava and magma**" (*Salty Solution*, t≈643). A
  species that settled on a volcano is allergic to it. Superb campaign irony.
- **Allergic to coral**: sneezing fits within seconds of entering a reef, forcing an abort
  (*Gemstone Reef*).
- Catches ordinary colds; is sick in bed in a frozen castle; recovers slowly ("**I'm still a
  little sick but I'm starting to feel better**").
- Dragons of all kinds get sick and infect each other — Icicle's cold passes to the fire
  dragon, who then can't sneeze safely (*Underground Rescue*, t≈297).
- **Physically capturable.** A single house cat imprisons a dragonet and a cat in an
  underground hole, in the dark, long enough that they "**blinked in the light because they'd
  been down in the dark for so long**". Rescue requires a shovel, a lantern, and six people.
- **Their homeland has two existential threats on the record**: eruption (fire) and Icicle's
  well-meant over-application of ice breath, which froze the entire island and displaced the
  population to a mouse's workshop. Fire and ice, both delivered by allies.

## 13. Relations with other peoples

| People | Attested relationship |
|---|---|
| **Cats** | Schoolmates, tail-petting friends, food-swap partners — and the one documented kidnapper (Gorp). Dragonets flee cats on sight after the incident. |
| **Mice** | Bakers, hatters, spouses, and quest partners. Also, historically, prey ("Could I eat that mouse?"). |
| **Wizards (Seeker)** | The dragonets' patron, ambulance, mediator, and transport. Dimitar's oldest friend; called by letter in emergencies. Seeker's potions and skyship are how dragonets leave their island. |
| **Big dragons (Icicle)** | Distant, benevolent, dangerously clumsy. Ice Fangsicle didn't know dragonets existed, then became their festival attraction, blowing ice into everyone's lemonade. |
| **Moles** | Diggers-for-hire; also the audience for the *Book of Dimitar the Dragon*. Dragonets are folklore to moles. |
| **Floofers** | Rescue clients; had "**heard about dragons but never met any. I'm glad you're nice.**" |
| **Humans / "kiddos"** | Giants who bring shovels, cupcakes, and cards. Dimitar had never eaten "people food" — "**We haven't had people food around here in a long time.**" |

# Part II — Roster of distinct dragonets

Every individual the transcripts identify as a dragonet/dragonette, plus the collectives.

### 1. Dimitar — the protagonist dragonet
*Spelled Dimitar / Dimatar / Dimetar / Demitar / Dematar / (once) "Dibbletarr" — all one
character. 22 stories, 2025-10-21 → 2026-08-02.*

- **Appearance**: small; **purple scaled skin, visibly faded with age**; rough scaly tail
  ("scaly and brown"); sharp teeth; snapping claws; small lizard-like nose; wings, arms, legs,
  tail. Wears nothing.
- **Age/status**: over 200 years old, full-grown, "older than most grown-ups", a legend with an
  ancient book written about him and read to mole children at bedtime.
- **Home**: a stone castle with a great bell tower on the flank of the volcano at the centre of
  the Perfectly Circular Island. Formerly of jungle Dragonet Town. Household includes Pouska,
  two orange dragonets, and dozens of resident dragonets.
- **Family**: married to **Hattie the Mouse** (they co-reside and argue constantly); mother is
  **Demora**, also called **Shiliko**; Zara wishes to join his family.
- **Deeds**: hatched from the smallest egg in a great mountain dragon nest and flew off to
  claim a jungle · was tasked by the Mayor of Dragonet Town to fetch ice, found the ice dragon
  instead, and brokered his friendship and his ice-breath party trick into the summer festival
  · wrote the distress letter that summoned Seeker when his castle turned out to sit on a
  volcano · sailed a pirate ship to Rainbow Forest Island and, from the crow's nest with a
  telescope, spotted land and found the buried Floofer Kaploofer · enrolled at Ginger Catasorry
  and completed a full school day (planet map, drawing, beads, songs, playground) · became
  friends with Ginger the cat over a mutual tail-petting · flew with Zudy over Strawberry
  Island until the teacher got dizzy · dived a blueberry ocean and came up with a lemon fish,
  drenching everybody · visited the Deep Deep Dark in a diving helmet and helped restore a
  lanternshark's light · went to Mars and the Moon, Star World, Book World, Cat World, and
  Gemstone Reef by potion · conducted the mozzarella **cheese train** to Cheese Beach · fought
  and reconciled with his wife over one fish and over his dragonet library · was bedridden with
  a lava allergy while his island froze solid · joined the rescue party for a trapped dragonet
  and rode the underground tunnel vehicle.
- **Voice**: fussy, self-important, hungry, forgetful, warm underneath. Trademark grumble;
  trademark line pattern is a proud correction followed by a request for fish.
- **Hooks**: he is not to be shown science books ("**you may borrow my science book as long as
  you don't let Dimitar see it**"); he is the only dragonet with genuine world experience,
  making him the natural patron/NPC-quest-giver for a dragonet party.

### 2. Pouska — the assistant
*The Giant Rescue on Volcano Island (2025-12-07).*
**Blue and green** dragonet. Dimitar's self-described assistant: "**That means I help him out
with everything. And boy does he need a lot of help with everything.**" Introduced faking a
giant's footsteps and a mean glare to frighten off strangers, then flipping to delight at a
friend's name. Guides visitors up the volcano by air, refuses to go the last stretch, and
abandons them at the door with "**Good luck with Dimitar, ha-ha!**" Perfect gatekeeper NPC.

### 3. Zudy (Zudy Dragon) — the visiting playmate
*The Flying Friends of Strawberry Island (2026-02-27).*
**Red and green — "just like a strawberry."** From **Strawberry Island**, and eats strawberries
"**for breakfast, lunch, and dinner**" (note: a dragonet on a fruit diet, the one documented
exception to fish). Announces themself at school playtime with "**I am Zudy Dragon, I've got
wings!**" when no one else can fly; recognised instantly — "**you must be a dragonet like me.**"
Flies aerobatics with Dimitar. Mother: **Allego**.

### 4. Zara (Zara Dragonette) — the rescue victim
*The Underground Rescue and Gorp's Lesson in Kindness (2026-08-02).*
A dragonet, female, **trapped underground in a dark hole with Ginger the cat** by Gorp the cat,
who wanted playmates and didn't know how to ask. Sends a written plea that arrives torn in
half. Calls for help for the length of an entire ocean crossing. Climbs out filthy, blinking at
the light. Flees Gorp, hides behind Seeker, then hugs and kisses him, tells him her name when
he finally asks politely, and teaches him to play tag. Asked to be his friend, she answers:
"**No, I wanna be part of Dimitar's family.**" Izzy's stated next story is "**Zara meets
Dimitar**" — an unwritten sequel, and an obvious campaign seed.

### 5. Zamuba — the messenger
*The Underground Rescue.* Named by Izzy. Arrives in a commotion carrying half the torn distress
note, tries to read it upside down ("**it says, zerba, derba, derba**"), then gets it: "**Help.
We're trapped underground.**" Shy on introduction, urgent on task: "**we have a big problem.**"

### 6. Zella — the second messenger
*The Underground Rescue.* Brought the **other half of the note in his mouth**. Supplies the
diagnosis and the ask: "**I think this is a message from one of our dragonet friends, and she's
trapped underground, back home, on the Circular Island. And we have to rescue them, and we
gotta go right now. … Can you help us, please?**"

### 7 & 8. The two orange dragonets — castle staff
*The Giant Rescue.* Summoned by a snap of Dimitar's claws; "**zipped right into the kitchen and
zipped back out with a bunch of snacks**" (bowls of dried fish). One returns with "**a little
tiny hammer**" to re-hang a fallen portrait. Silent, fast, competent. Listed in canon as
"Orange Dragonets — Dimitar's castle assistants who serve snacks and help around the castle."

### 9. The Mayor of Dragonet Town
*Dimitar and the Castle of Ice Fangsicle.* Unseen but structurally important: the sole attested
dragonet office-holder. "**By the Mayor of that dragonet town, I was given a task. And the task
was to bring back a good amount of ice for our summer festival to cool down our delicious
drinks. And I took this task very seriously.**" Governs the jungle town; issues quests;
festival logistics are matters of state.

### 10. Demora / Shiliko — Dimitar's mother
Two names, one role, both from Dad's and Izzy's narration. Walks Dimitar to school and flies
beside him; introduces herself to other mothers and arranges cross-island playdates; arrives at
day's end for pickup; at the birthday party she is the one who says "**It's time to go**" and
prompts the gift-giving.

### 11. Allego — Zudy's mother
*The Flying Friends of Strawberry Island.* "**My name is Allego, Zudy's mom.**" Comes to the
playground at pickup, meets Demora, agrees to the playdate. Strawberry Island resident.

### 12. The Dragonettes (collective)
*The Dragonettes and the Rainbow Forest Quest (2026-02-25).* The name Izzy and Dad use when
treating dragonets as an adventuring band rather than individuals. Canonical group description:
"a group of small dragons who embark on adventures and enjoy eating fish and ice." Their
brainstormed itinerary — Square Island (where everything is square, home of Farmer Wobble and
his square carrots), the Rainbow Forest, the Floofer Cloud, "an adventure about fishies" — is
effectively an unrun dragonet campaign outline.

### 13. The dragonets of the Circular Island (population)
"**Dozens of little dragonets of all different colors**" in Dimitar's castle; more in village
houses with gardens; seen napping, gardening, drinking, and chasing each other through the air.
They deliberate as a crowd, panic as a crowd, and resolve as a crowd. Every one of them attends
Seeker's party in the final story: "**It's every dragonet from Demitar's Island.**"

### 14. The dragonet who drowned a book (unnamed)
*Book World.* Read a borrowed book while flying over the ocean, dropped it, and the recovered
volume "**all just turned to mush**". Cause of Dimitar's library policy. Never named — an easy
NPC to develop.

### Ambiguous case: Senexa
*The Security Cats and the Rainbow Party (2026-07-01).* Self-described: "**I'm the dragon friend
that you met at Castlerock. I have red belly and a green back, green wings.**" Named Senexa,
brings tuna, comes from **Strawberry Island** — the same island and near-identical red/green
colouring as Zudy the dragonet. Canon calls Senexa "a dragon" rather than a dragonet, so treat
as **probable dragonet, unconfirmed** (kin to Zudy is my inference, not canon).

# Part III — The big dragons, for contrast

Not dragonets. Useful precisely because they define what dragonets are not.

- **Icicle** (a.k.a. **Ice Fangsicle**, "Icecycle") — "**a giant ancient white dragon the size
  of a school bus**"; **497 years old**; **freezing breath** that turns his exhalation into
  "**thousands of shards of ice**"; lives alone in an ice castle of "**massive blocks of ice**"
  on the highest peak; obsessive about keeping it "**neat and clean and sparkly polished**";
  eats only frozen fish and makes "fish-sicles"; seasons his food from a salt box "**almost the
  size of this entire castle**"; profoundly lonely ("**I can make ice with my breath … but I
  can't make friends**"); **married to Uncle Bill**; catches colds that cost him his breath
  weapon; froze an entire inhabited island *by accident, while trying to help*. The archive's
  best example of well-meaning catastrophic power.
- **The fire dragon** — unnamed, offstage, sick with Icicle's cold. "**You don't wanna be near
  a fire dragon when he sneezes … 'cause he sneezes a big fireball. Fwoosh.**"
- **Saluska** — "**a huge, fluffy, blue sleep dragon**" with "**big, droopy eyes**", soft fur
  and a deep voice, ruling Sleepy World; manifests a custom perfect bed for anyone who asks;
  her mere presence makes visitors drowsy.
- **Jar Jar** — a **red dragon** who "**won't eat you**" only on assurance; states his politics
  plainly: "**I like nice girls, but I don't like bad boy. I don't like boys.**" Gets a lecture
  from Seeker on second chances.
- **The Dragoness** — the mother-dragon of the great mountain nest in Dimitar's origin book.
  Named once, never seen.
- **Komodo dragons** — mundane 10-foot reptiles in a zoo and in Indonesia, measured in
  "Isabellas", which refuse to get along and want only beef. Not dragons at all in-world, and
  a good reminder that the archive distinguishes real animals from dragon-people.

# Part IV — What canon does *not* say

Free invention space, with the reason each gap matters:

- **No breath weapon, no spellcasting, no supernatural ability of any kind** for dragonets. If
  you give them one, you are adding to canon, not extending it.
- **No hoard, no gold-lust, no treasure.** The only coveted objects are books, beads, shiny
  glitter, and fish. (The one gold reference is *structural* — gold woven into a nest.)
- **No measurements.** No height, wingspan, or weight for any dragonet.
- **No lifecycle detail** beyond hatching and "we live a long time": no clutch sizes, no
  juvenile stage, no death, no elders' rites.
- **No religion, no magic tradition, no writing system, no currency, no law, no army.** One
  mayor, one castle-holder, one school.
- **No named dragonet homeland beyond two settlements** (Circular Island; jungle Dragonet
  Town), though Strawberry Island hosts at least two.
- **No conflict between dragonets and any other people.** The single antagonist in the entire
  dragonet corpus is one lonely house cat.
- **Naming pattern worth exploiting**: of six individually named dragonets, four begin with
  **Z** (Zara, Zamuba, Zella, Zudy) and most end in a vowel (Pouska, Demora, Shiliko, Allego,
  Senexa). "Dimitar" is the outlier — fitting for the one who left.

# Part V — Campaign hooks that are already in the transcripts

The archive contains its own D&D brief. On 2026-08-02, Dad opens with:

> "**My friends that I make up adventure stories with, we play a game called Dungeons and
> Dragons. We are gonna start soon a new adventure all about dragonets, and I thought it might
> be fun to have you help come up with ideas for it. … The main thing we need to do is come up
> with an interesting problem for the story that the dragonets need to solve.**"

Izzy's answer is a complete adventure premise, unprompted:

> "**It's that they get somewhere stuck, and someone puts them somewhere where they can't get
> out, and maybe they're lost, and they escape while that person is sleeping.**"
> — and on the villain: "**It has to be Gorp, 'cause he's very mean.**"

The story then played it out and, crucially, **subverted its own villain**: Gorp's motive turns
out to be "**I just wanted them to play with me, but they kept running away, so I had to keep
them somewhere**", and the resolution is teaching him a kind voice, how to ask a name, and how
to play tag. That is the tonal key for the whole setting: **antagonists in dragonet stories are
lonely, not evil, and the win condition is social.**

Other ready-made hooks, each straight from a transcript:

1. **The volcano is only postponed.** Icicle froze the magma once, by accident freezing
   everything else. "**Just let me know next time you think that volcano might erupt and I can
   try to help without freezing everything.**" The dragonet homeland sits on a timer with an
   unreliable fix.
2. **Dimitar's castle was physically relocated** by an embiggened wizard, and the *choice of
   where to put it was never made* — Izzy got tired and the story broke off mid-lift. A castle
   in the air, unresolved, since 2025-12-07.
3. **Zara meets Dimitar** — the sequel Izzy announced and never told. An orphaned rescue victim
   petitions to join the ruling household.
4. **The lost book in the ocean** — a dragonet book was drowned and pulped; its dust later
   became a potion ingredient that opened Book World. Dragonet literature is literally
   world-opening material.
5. **The Mayor's errand structure** — dragonet quests come from municipal need (get ice for the
   festival) and end in diplomacy, not combat. A dragonet party is a party of couriers,
   negotiators, and caterers.
6. **Nobody knows what a dragonet is.** An ancient dragon, a floofer, and a book of the world
   have all never heard of them. A dragonet abroad is a xenological event.
7. **The lava allergy** — dragonets cannot personally investigate the thing most likely to kill
   them. They must send, hire, or persuade someone else to go down the volcano.
8. **Square Island, the Rainbow Forest, and the Floofer Cloud** were explicitly brainstormed as
   dragonet destinations and never used (2026-02-25). Pre-approved unexplored map.
