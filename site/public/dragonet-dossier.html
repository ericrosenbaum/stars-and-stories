Part I

What the transcripts establish

Thirteen topics with direct evidence. Nothing here is extrapolated except where labelled.

01Taxonomy

A dragonet is a small kind of dragon, not a young dragon

This is the load-bearing fact in the archive, and it is stated flatly in two separate stories five weeks apart.

The mistake is played as a recurring social indignity: big creatures read dragonets as hatchlings, and Dimitar corrects them every time. Note too that an ancient ice dragon had never heard of them — "What's a dragonet?" — and neither had a Floofer, or a talking book of the world. Dragonets are obscure, insular, regional. A dragonet abroad is a xenological event.

02Size

Never measured, only compared

No dragonet is ever given a height, wingspan, or weight. What canon supplies instead is a comparison ladder — which is arguably more useful at a table.

The ordering is canon. The widths are illustrative only — the outlined rung is inference from behaviour (a dragonet fits cat-school furniture and can be imprisoned by one house cat). Embiggened Seeker is explicitly "three times as tall as the entire castle."

  • Beside a great dragon: "a giant ancient white dragon the size of a school bus, he dwarfed me. I had to look straight up into his face" — and the giant must bring "his head down low to the ground to squint close at Dimitar."
  • Beside a wizard: embiggened Seeker must warn, "I don't want to step on any tiny dragonets."
  • Beside a human: "you're really tall compared to us. Maybe you could help us by, I don't know, finding us a shovel or something."
  • Beside a mouse: one fish is a shared meal for two, and they argue over who gets the head.

Practical read: small — roughly cat-to-badger mass — in a world where mice and cats are people. Their stone castles have full-size doors because the whole island is built at dragonet scale.

03Lifespan

Two centuries, and small forever

  • A dragonet's small stature is terminal, not developmental.
  • Age shows in colour: "you can see mine's purple, although it's faded a little bit because I am, to be honest, very old."
  • The big dragons run on the same clock — Icicle is "497 years old, but once I was four when I was a tiny little dragon" — so dragonets are long-lived and small, not a short-lived offshoot.
  • Contradiction Yet dragonets have living mothers who collect them from school and arrange playdates. Canon never reconciles this. My recommendation for play: dragonet society treats age as irrelevant to status and keeps its members enrolled in institutions forever.
04Origin

Hatched as the runt, in a story read out of an ancient book

The only hatching account is framed as history: Papa Mole finds "the ancient book about Dimitar" dusty in his attic, cover art of "a tiny purple dragon named Dimitar," and reads it to his children at bedtime. So in-world, Dimitar is a legend with published books about him — which is how a 200-year-old can also be a newborn on the page.

  • A "great dragon nest" high in cold mountains, full of "many broken eggshells from the dragons who had already hatched" — and one egg "smaller than all the rest. Much, much smaller." Dragonets may hatch as the runt of an ordinary dragon clutch.
  • Nest materials, Izzy's invention and the best image in the archive: "strawberries and feathers, with gold … gold, sparkly glitter dragon branches … and sea grass and dragon pests. Dragon pests are a kind of rock."
  • Out of the shell comes "the little tiny purple face of a very smart and very mad-looking little dragon," speaking "baby dragon language" that translates as "I need some snacks right away!"
  • No parent present; opinions about food already formed; flight untested but assumed — "Well, I think I can fly. I've never tried it yet" — and it works first try.
  • Hatchlings leave at once and choose their own territory: "I can't stay here in the nest. I have to go fly and find a new place to have a nest." He picked a jungle off a map.

He was wrong, and he never found out until he stopped growing. A dragonet hatchling may not know it is a dragonet.

05Body

Scales, teeth, claws, a superb nose — and nothing that breathes

  • Bare scales, no clothing. "I and my dragonet friends, we all just flap around in our scaly skin" — in a world where mice run hat shops. The scales shed water: "You're just wearing your scales and those just drip dry right off, don't they?"
  • Tail: rough, scaly, brown-purple — and tail-petting is the standard greeting ritual with cats. He notes he "haven't had a tail pet in a long time."
  • Sharp teeth used as tools. "You know dragonettes have very sharp teeth. I could just bite it. Here, watch this. Chomp!" — biting a bead-string to length in a school craft lesson.
  • Claws snapped as a summons. "Dimitar snapped one of his claws like this — snap, snap" and two orange dragonet servants sprint for snacks. He does it again to muster the whole castle. Snapping is command authority.
  • A nose better than a wizard's, described as lizard-like: he smells a bakery from another street, and Seeker defers to him — "You have a better nose than I do, Dimitar."
  • A wing substitutes for a hand. Asked to raise his hand in class: "I'll raise my wing."

In an archive where ice dragons freeze islands and a fire dragon "sneezes a big fireball," no dragonet ever breathes anything.

No breath weapon, no spellcasting, no supernatural ability of any kind. This is the sharpest design line in the material: dragonets are the dragons without the dragon powers. Their only offensive capability is bluff — see topic 08.

06Flight

Fast, vertical, playful, and short-ranged

  • The verb is always flap: "They flap! Flap, flap, flap, flap, flap, flap, flap. That means flying."
  • Faster than ground transport — asked "who is faster? The flying dragonettes or the driving cats?" Izzy settles it: "the dragons are faster," and they overfly the cats' car en route to school.
  • Casual and vertical: Dimitar "flapped up high into the air and circled around once, whoosh! all the way around the castle, and then zoomed off down the mountain" — to fetch a snack.
  • Hunting stoop: over a blueberry ocean "he flew up high and then dove straight down and with a splash … swooped back up again" holding a lemon fish.
  • Can ferry a passenger — a mouse rides his back on his very first flight.
  • Aerial play is the standard recreation: village dragonets are seen "playing in the air, flapping around and chasing each other and laughing," and two of them fly aerobatics until the schoolteacher gets dizzy.
Range is a real limit. Crossing to the mountains, "it was a long, long journey and my wings became very tired" — and for the return leg he asks to ride on the big dragon's back. Long distances are otherwise done by pirate ship, the wizard's skyship, or a magic flying ladder. A dragonet expedition is a logistics problem.
07Diet

Fish — absolutely, comically, and as a system of etiquette

The most consistently attested cultural fact in the dragonet corpus.

  • The staple is dried fish. "These are some little bowls full of delicious crunchy things that we dragonets like to eat. They're actually dried fish … It's quite delicious, and a little salty. Make sure you get some water too."
  • Exclusivity, admitted. Offered cat kibble: "Crunch crunch. Blech! This is disgusting! How do you eat this stuff? I mean, I only like fish, I guess." And: "Save some for me. That's all I really eat."
  • A whole fish-derived cuisine: sardine cupcakes with salty sardine filling, fish coffee with milk, fish crackers, a fish sandwich, fish juice, fish sauce, fish-flavoured sprinkles, and a requested herring roll and salmon-flavoured cake.
  • Fish anatomy is etiquette. Splitting one fish: "Okay, I get the head." / "the head is delicious but you can have the head and I'll have the tail." The grievance that opens the Book World story is that Hattie "ate all of the body and the tail," leaving Dimitar "only had the head left to eat." Head and tail are separately desirable; taking both is a real insult.
  • Frank predators. First reaction to a coral reef: "I wonder if we could eat some of these fish, they look delicious." As a hatchling he asked outright, "Hey, I like mice. Could I eat that mouse?" — worth remembering, given he later marries one.
  • Ice is a delicacy, and the cause of the archive's first dragonet quest: fetch ice "for our summer festival to cool down our delicious drinks."
  • Salt is prized — the island rescue ends in "a huge feast of all sorts of delicious salty snacks" — though Dimitar objects to salt as a tool: "With seasoning? Everything's going to taste like salt!"
  • They will nibble the scenery. As conductor of a cheese train: "You may take a little nibble, but don't eat up the whole cheese train, 'cause then it won't be able to take passengers somewhere."
08Temperament

Grumpy, proud, possessive, forgetful, panicky — and quick to forgive

The tonal core, and very usable at a table.

First contact with a lone dragonet should always be a threat display that collapses on a password.

  • Vanity. An earthquake knocks a picture off the wall: "Oh, that was one of my favorite pictures of me. Could somebody put it back up, please?"
  • Possessiveness, with a stated wound underneath. "You can't have any of them, Hattie!" — and the reason: "I lent one of my dragonet books to my dragonet friend and he was trying to read it while he was flying … and he dropped it in the ocean! … I even dove down and found the book but it was all wet and then it all just turned to mush."
  • Forgetfulness. Mid-quest: "Oh, oh yeah. Wait, what's our mission again?" His assistant's assessment: "I help him out with everything. And boy does he need a lot of help with everything."
  • Curiosity under the grumbling. "This Catasorry school is a lot of work, but I like learning."
  • Flight, not fight. Facing the cat who imprisoned them, Zara and Ginger "just ran … and hid behind his back" — then hugged him once he apologised.

Compare the emergency chorus a story later: "Hey, no, what do you… What should we do? Oh, no, don't bother everyone. I'm not sure who. I'm scared. Wait, but, but we can figure this out. But no, no, help. We should go home right now." Dragonets panic as a crowd and resolve as a crowd.

09Society

Villages, gardens, a bell tower, and one mayor

The Perfectly Circular Island is the dragonet homeland of the current era: a perfect circle of land with one central mountain that is really a volcano, sparkling yellow beaches, forests, little rivers, and dragonet villages. Dimitar's stone castle sits on the volcano's flank, its giant tower bell doubling as his doorbell.

  • An agrarian village culture with a fortified centre, gardens, drinks on the porch, and a household staff. Dozens live in Dimitar's castle alone; Icicle refers to "all the villages of the dragonets that live on his island with him."
  • Household hierarchy is real but informal. Pouska is the assistant and escort who hands guests off with "Okay, this is as far as I take you. Good luck with Dimitar, ha-ha!" Two orange dragonets are kitchen staff who also do carpentry.
  • Dragonet Town, in a jungle "in a very warm part of the world," is Dimitar's earlier home, and has a Mayor who assigns civic quests plus a summer festival with cold drinks. Two documented settlement types: warm-climate jungle town, temperate island village.
  • Emergency comms are physical, fragile, and garbled. One story is triggered by a letter on purple paper; another by a distress note that arrives torn in half and carried in two dragonets' mouths.
  • Doorbells are a civilisational obsession. Every dragonet home has one, ringing one is the standard story opening, and Dimitar's tower bell is loud enough to annoy him: "Who's making all that racket with my doorbell?"
  • Attested governance, in full: one mayor, one castle-holder, and a lot of shouting. No kings, priests, armies, courts, or currency anywhere.
10Kinship

Mothers, playdate diplomacy, and interspecies marriage

  • Mothers are present and named — and named twice, differently: "Dimetar's mom's name is Demora" and "said Dimatar's mom, Shiliko." Contradiction She walks him to school, collects him at day's end, and negotiates with other mothers.
  • Cross-island playdate diplomacy, verbatim: "We live on Circular Island. What island does your family live on?" / "Strawberry Island." / "Do you think we could come over sometime for a playdate?" / "Of course."

Dimitar is married to Hattie the Mouse; they co-reside in his castle and argue constantly, and Seeker reminds them mid-fight to "remember that you love each other." There is precedent among the big dragons too: Uncle Bill is married to Icicle the ice dragon, confirmed by Izzy. Interspecies marriage is normal and unremarked.

Dragonet kinship looks adoptive and affiliative — a household you petition into.

11Schooling

The strongest institution in the material

Dragonets and cats attend school together. It is called Cadasori / Ginger Catasorry / "Ginger school," and Izzy is always the teacher. Inconsistent Its location wobbles: the island is called "the hallowed grounds of the school," Dimitar and his mother fly there and land in a parking lot, and Izzy at one point says flatly that it is "in my room."

  • Curriculum, all attested: the planet map (continents and planets), drawing, bead counting and colour sorting, beadwork, circle time with songs, nap time, snack time — kibble for cats, fish for dragonets — lunch, and outside time.
  • Multi-species architecture. "There's steps and a handrail or wing-rail. There's a handrail and a wing-rail and a paw-rail." Dad supplies the design principle: "That's so convenient for all the different kind of creatures that want to be on the playground."
  • Institutional texture: lunchboxes required except on "Buy-days"; music Wednesdays; placemats chosen from a cart; nap bag, mat, and stuffie (Dimitar's is a purple cat named Sanina); the teacher covers him with "his blanket with dragons"; the teacher's flashlight is not for students.
  • Dragonets are provincial. Shown a world map: "I've never heard of any of these places … I just live on the Perfectly Circular Island," and later, "there's no circular island on here." A dragonet's mental map is his island.
  • Literacy is real and prestigious. Dimitar keeps a library of books specifically "about dragonets"; Hattie the Mouse, by contrast, "didn't know how to read yet." There is a dragonet literature, and losing a book is a genuine tragedy.
12Vulnerability

Allergic to their own volcano

A species that settled on a volcano is allergic to it. The DM consequence is excellent: dragonets cannot personally investigate the thing most likely to kill them — they must send, hire, or persuade someone else down the mountain.

  • Also allergic to coral: sneezing fits within seconds of entering a reef, forcing an abort.
  • Catches ordinary colds; recovers slowly ("I'm still a little sick but I'm starting to feel better"). Dragons of every kind infect one another — Icicle's cold passes to the fire dragon, who then cannot sneeze safely.
  • Physically capturable. One house cat imprisons a dragonet and a cat in an underground hole, in the dark, long enough that they "blinked in the light because they'd been down in the dark for so long." Rescue took a shovel, a lantern, and six people.
  • The homeland has two existential threats on record — eruption, and an ally's over-applied ice breath that froze the whole island and displaced the population into a mouse's workshop. Fire and ice, both delivered by friends.

Part III

The big dragons, for contrast

Not dragonets — and useful precisely because they define what dragonets are not.

Contrastfive dragons & one lizard

Icicle, also called Ice Fangsicle

"A giant ancient white dragon the size of a school bus." 497 years old. Freezing breath that turns his exhalation into "thousands of shards of ice." Lives alone in a castle of "massive blocks of ice" on the highest peak, obsessive about keeping it "neat and clean and sparkly polished." Eats only frozen fish and makes fish-sicles; seasons his food from a salt box "almost the size of this entire castle." Married to Uncle Bill. Catches colds that cost him his breath weapon. And he once froze an entire inhabited island by accident, while trying to help — the archive's best study in well-meaning catastrophic power.

The fire dragon

Unnamed, offstage, sick with Icicle's cold. "You don't wanna be near a fire dragon when he sneezes … 'cause he sneezes a big fireball. Fwoosh."

Saluska

"A huge, fluffy, blue sleep dragon" with "big, droopy eyes," soft fur and a deep voice, ruling Sleepy World. Manifests a custom perfect bed for anyone who asks; her mere presence makes visitors drowsy.

Jar Jar

A red dragon who "won't eat you" — but only on assurance. States his politics plainly: "I like nice girls, but I don't like bad boy. I don't like boys." Receives a lecture from Seeker on second chances.

The Dragoness

Mother-dragon of the great mountain nest in Dimitar's origin book. Named once, never seen.

Komodo dragons

Mundane ten-foot reptiles in a zoo and in Indonesia, measured in "Isabellas," which refuse to get along and want only beef. Not dragons at all in-world — a useful reminder that this setting distinguishes real animals from dragon-people.

Part IV

What canon does not say

Free invention space — with the reason each gap matters.

Silencesinvent freely here
  • No breath weapon, no spellcasting, no supernatural ability. Give a dragonet one and you are adding to canon, not extending it.
  • No hoard, no gold-lust, no treasure. The coveted objects are books, beads, glitter, and fish. The one gold reference is structural — gold woven into a nest.
  • No measurements. No height, wingspan, or weight for any dragonet, ever.
  • No lifecycle detail beyond hatching and "we live a long time": no clutch sizes, no juvenile stage, no death, no rites for elders.
  • No religion, magic tradition, writing system, currency, law, or army. One mayor, one castle-holder, one school.
  • No homeland beyond two settlements — the Circular Island and jungle Dragonet Town — though Strawberry Island hosts at least two dragonets.
  • No conflict between dragonets and any other people. The single antagonist in the entire dragonet corpus is one lonely house cat.
A naming pattern worth exploiting. Of six individually named dragonets, four begin with Z — Zara, Zamuba, Zella, Zudy — and most of the rest end in a vowel: Pouska, Demora, Shiliko, Allego, Senexa. "Dimitar" is the outlier, which is fitting for the one who left.
Part V

Hooks already in the transcripts

The archive contains its own campaign brief — and its own answer to it.

The brief2026-08-02

The answer came back as a complete adventure premise, unprompted:

The story then played it out and subverted its own villain: Gorp's motive turns out to be "I just wanted them to play with me, but they kept running away, so I had to keep them somewhere," and the resolution is teaching him a kind voice, how to ask a name, and how to play tag.

Antagonists in dragonet stories are lonely, not evil, and the win condition is social.

Hookseight, ready to run
  1. The volcano is only postponed. Icicle froze the magma once, freezing everything else by accident. "Just let me know next time you think that volcano might erupt and I can try to help without freezing everything." The homeland sits on a timer with an unreliable fix.
  2. Dimitar's castle was lifted by an embiggened wizard and never put down. The choice of where to set it was never made — Izzy got tired and the story broke off mid-lift. Unresolved since 2025-12-07.
  3. "Zara meets Dimitar" — the sequel Izzy announced and never told. An orphaned rescue victim petitions to join the ruling household.
  4. The lost book in the ocean. A drowned, pulped dragonet book was later ground into dust that opened a doorway to Book World. Dragonet literature is literally world-opening material.
  5. The Mayor's errand structure. Dragonet quests come from municipal need — fetch ice for the festival — and end in diplomacy, not combat. A dragonet party is a party of couriers, negotiators, and caterers.
  6. Nobody knows what a dragonet is. An ancient dragon, a Floofer, and a talking book of the world have all never heard of them.
  7. The lava allergy forces delegation: dragonets cannot go down their own volcano. They must hire or persuade someone who can.
  8. Square Island, the Rainbow Forest, and the Floofer Cloud were explicitly brainstormed as dragonet destinations and never used. Pre-approved unexplored map.
